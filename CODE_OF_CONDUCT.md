# TGF Code of Conduct

**Status:** Community participation policy for the TASIMAS GUI Framework.

TGF aims to be a technically rigorous and welcoming project. Participation in its
repository, issues, pull requests, reviews, project discussions, and official project
activities is expected to be respectful, constructive, and safe.

This policy is adapted from the principles and graduated enforcement approach of the
[Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/).
It is written for this repository rather than reproduced verbatim.

## 1. Our commitment

Everyone participating in the TGF community should be able to take part without harassment
or discrimination. Treat people fairly regardless of personal characteristics, identity,
background, nationality, experience, education, disability, or viewpoint.

Technical disagreement is normal and welcome. Critique ideas, code, evidence, and decisions;
do not attack the person presenting them.

## 2. Expected behavior

Examples of behavior that help the community:

- Be considerate, patient, and respectful.
- Explain technical criticism with reasons and evidence.
- Accept that contributors have different levels of experience and different perspectives.
- Give feedback that is specific, relevant, and focused on improving the work.
- Acknowledge mistakes, correct them, and learn from them.
- Respect project scope, review processes, and the time of other contributors.
- Protect private information and obtain permission before sharing personal material.

## 3. Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- Harassment, intimidation, threats, stalking, or unwanted repeated contact.
- Discriminatory, demeaning, insulting, or hateful language or conduct.
- Personal attacks, deliberate humiliation, trolling, or sustained disruption of technical
  discussions.
- Sexualized comments, imagery, attention, or advances in project spaces.
- Publishing or threatening to publish another person's private information without
  permission.
- Impersonation, malicious manipulation of project discussions, or retaliation against
  someone who raises a concern in good faith.
- Other conduct that a reasonable person would consider abusive or seriously inappropriate
  in a professional technical community.

A disagreement, a good-faith technical criticism, or a respectful request for evidence is
not by itself a Code of Conduct violation.

## 4. Scope

This policy applies to project-controlled spaces, including this repository's issues,
pull requests, reviews, discussions (if enabled), and project documentation. It also
applies when someone is acting as an official TGF/TASIMAS representative in a public or
offline setting.

## 5. Reporting a concern

Do not report a sensitive conduct incident by opening a public issue or by posting
personal information in a public comment.

For an incident attached to a GitHub issue, pull request, or comment:

1. Open the **Report content** action for the relevant item.
2. Choose **Report to repository admins** when GitHub offers that option.
3. Provide a concise, factual description and relevant links or context.

That route is available only when repository administrators have enabled GitHub's
reported-content feature. If **Report to repository admins** is not offered, use
**Report abuse to GitHub Support** instead. You can also report an abusive user or
organization through GitHub's profile-level reporting controls.

The repository does not currently publish a separate conduct-report email address.
Do not guess an address or send a report to an unrelated public issue. The repository
owner should enable GitHub's reported-content feature in repository settings and review
incoming reports there. The setting is outside version-controlled repository files.

Reports should be factual and made in good faith. Include links, approximate dates, and
relevant context where possible; avoid forwarding unrelated private information. Reports
will be handled as discreetly as the investigation allows. Absolute confidentiality or a
particular response time cannot be guaranteed.

## 6. Enforcement and corrective action

Project maintainers are responsible for reviewing reports brought to their attention and
for taking fair, proportionate action. They may remove or edit material, close or lock a
conversation, request that behavior stop, or restrict participation where appropriate.
People affected by a decision should receive an explanation when it is safe and practical
to provide one.

Depending on severity, context, impact, and prior behavior, corrective action may include:

1. **Correction:** a private explanation of the concern and a request to stop or change the
   behavior.
2. **Warning:** a formal warning that identifies the conduct and explains that repetition
   may lead to restrictions.
3. **Temporary restriction:** a time-limited restriction from some or all project spaces.
4. **Permanent restriction:** removal of access or a permanent ban from project spaces.

Maintainers may skip steps when the seriousness or safety risk of an incident requires it.
Decisions should be based on the available evidence, applied consistently, and kept
proportionate. A person directly involved in a report should not be the sole reviewer of
that report when another suitable maintainer is available.

## 7. Retaliation and privacy

Retaliation against someone for raising a concern in good faith, participating in a review,
or cooperating with an investigation is prohibited. Do not publish a reporter's identity,
private messages, contact details, or other sensitive information without permission,
except where disclosure is required for a fair investigation or by law.
